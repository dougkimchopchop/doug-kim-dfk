Slow Down Time
Slow Down Time by Packing the Day With New Memories
Sep 18, 2026 · @Doug

The brain judges how long something took by how much memory it recorded, so living in a novel way fills the day with memories and makes it feel longer. The clock doesn't change; your memory of the day does.

More memories written, slower time

Consciousness mostly probes recent memory to ask “what just happened?”, so a richly recorded moment feels long. His rule: duration is “just a matter of how much memory you can pull up.” The amygdala triggers this by telling the brain to focus on what's happening right now.

Novelty is what makes the brain write

Novel experience makes the brain record heavily; repetition doesn't. For a kid every day is new, so a summer full of camp and hiking is packed with landmarks and feels enormous. Adults have a well-developed model of the world, so little is new, less gets recorded, and time seems to speed up.

Putting it into practice

These follow from his points; they aren't his direct advice.

  • Swap repeats for firsts. New places, people, skills and routes give the brain something to record.
  • Build landmarks. Aim for days where “what did you do?” has a list of distinct moments as its answer.
  • Stay in the present. Attention on what's happening now, not tomorrow's to-do list, is the state the amygdala forces in an emergency.
  • Judge it in hindsight. The fuller day feels longer when you look back on it, not while it's happening.
