Atomic habits

Kill This Wasted Life

You have four plans, a 16-habit tracker, a brain protocol and a calendar scheduled to the half hour. You don't have a planning problem. You have a doing problem, and every new system is another way to avoid doing.

The rule Make the habit so small you have no excuse. Then never miss twice.
The four laws
Cue
Make it obvious. If you have to remember it, you won't.
Craving
Make it attractive. Pair it with something you already want.
Response
Make it easy. Two minutes. Shoes on, not a 5K.
Reward
Make it satisfying. Mark it done the second it's done.

For bad habits, flip all four: invisible, unattractive, hard, unsatisfying. Don't analyze why you do them. Remove the cue and add friction.

The rules
1
Never miss twice. Missing once is an accident. Missing twice is the new habit. The day after a miss, do the two-minute version no matter what.
2
Bad day = minimum day. One sentence, ten push-ups, one ask. That counts. Zero doesn't.
3
No new systems. Every hour spent redesigning the tracker is an hour you didn't write, shoot or ask.
4
Don't analyze a miss. No journaling about why. Log it, do the next rep.
5
Set up the night before. Camera charged and by the door. Gym clothes on the chair. Writing doc already open.
6
Done beats good. Ugly and posted beats perfect and in drafts.
7
Count outputs, not intentions. Pieces published, photos posted, asks sent. Nothing else goes on the scoreboard.
