Furious Action Plan + Friction Maxxing Plan

Furious action plan

Become ferocious at the exact moment you usually switch away. Catch yourself reaching for the exit. “I should get organized first.” There’s your resistance. Turn toward it. Get your hands back on the fucking work.

Use CBT at the decision point
  1. Catch the thought. Say the exact words. “I need a clear head before I can write this.” Get the thought out where you can examine it.
  2. Turn it into a prediction. “If I start with a scattered head, I won’t produce anything I can use.” Make the claim specific. Give yourself something to test.
  3. Run the experiment. Open the piece. Draft the next scene for 20 minutes. Start with one thing you actually saw. Feel scattered and write anyway. Then inspect the work. Did you get a usable sentence? Did the scene come into focus? Put the prediction up against what you actually did.
  4. Return the instant you notice you’ve switched. Close the tab. Hands back on the work. Feel the pull to leave and take the next step anyway. “There’s the resistance. I’m fucking going in.”
Give the hard work a finish line

Pick one thing to finish today that changes your situation. Define it with a verb you can verify.

  • Writing. “Publish the essay.” Still drafting? “Finish the draft through the last paragraph.”
  • Money. “Open the IRS notices, write down the tax years and balances, and make the call.”
  • Photography. “Make the edit, sequence the photographs, and export the set.”

Go straight at the part you’ve been working around. Keep revising the opening? Write the ending. Keep organizing the tax folder? Open the notice. Keep tweaking the edit? Make the final fucking cut.

Your cliff jump today is the first consequential action you’ve been postponing. Put it in front of you. Give it 20 minutes. Use what happens to decide your next move. Keep driving toward the finish line.

At the end of the day, write two lines. What did I finish? What thought did I act through?

I can feel the resistance and fucking love it and run towards the cliff and the friction and discomfort. Do the hard thing. Run to the pain. Finish it.

Friction maxxing plan

Build a hunger for demanding work. Want the challenge. Go looking for the satisfaction inside the effort. Finish something, feel what you’ve earned, and come back wanting another round.

Key tenet
Focus on tomorrow’s reward instead of today’s discomfort. Want that reward so fucking much that you run toward the friction. Crave the challenge. Love the struggle. Do the hard thing. Finish it.
  1. Rehearse wanting it. Recall a demanding experience you loved. Camera in hand, completely absorbed. A difficult sentence finally taking shape. Bring back the feeling of being fully engaged. “I fucking want that again. Let me into this work.” Open the task and begin.
  2. Make friction your cue to approach. Boredom. Uncertainty. Reluctance. There’s the moment to move. “Here’s where I lean in. Here’s where I get stronger at doing this.” Open the difficult document. Write the awkward paragraph. Begin the conversation. Follow the feeling with action.
  3. Find pleasure inside the struggle. Pay attention as the problem starts making sense, the decision becomes clear, or the sentence gets sharper. Catch those moments while they happen. “Fuck yes. I’m figuring it out. I want to take this further.” Build your appetite around satisfaction you actually feel.
  4. Reward staying while you are staying. Feel the urge to escape. Complete the next step. Register what you just did. “I felt the pull and stayed with it. Fuck yes. Another step.” Take satisfaction in holding your attention and moving the work forward.
  5. Make tomorrow’s reward vivid. See the essay published. The application submitted. The listing live. Imagine waking up with that obligation finished and that opportunity open. “I fucking want that tomorrow. I’m putting the work in today.” Take the action that earns it.
  6. Savor completion until it registers. Look at the finished work. Let the relief, pride, and possibilities land. “I went toward the resistance. I worked through it. I finished the fucking thing.” Record what you accomplished and what felt satisfying. Give yourself a real experience to want again.
  7. Feed the appetite through repetition. Choose another meaningful challenge tomorrow. Raise the demand as your capacity grows. Eat, sleep, move, recover, and return ready to work. “Give me the next challenge. I want to see what I can finish.”

Some sessions will feel rough throughout. Record that honestly. Keep finding the moments when struggle becomes absorbing, when effort feels satisfying, and when finishing makes you hungry to do it again.

Finish it. Feel it. Come back hungry.
