Furious Action Plan + Friction Maxxing Plan

Furious action plan

Get ferocious at the moment you usually switch away. Catch the thought that makes escape feel reasonable. “I should get organized first.” That’s your cue. Turn toward the fucking work.

Use CBT at the decision point
  1. Catch the thought. Use its exact words. “I need a clear head before I can write this.” Write it down in one sentence.
  2. Make it testable. “If I write while scattered, I won’t produce one usable paragraph.” Name what you predict will happen.
  3. Test it through action. Open the piece. Write the next scene for 20 minutes. Start with something you actually saw. Let the discomfort come along. “I can feel this and fucking work.”
  4. Check the evidence. Read what you produced. Was the prediction accurate? Write a conclusion that fits the result. “Starting was uncomfortable. I still produced a paragraph I can use.”
  5. Return immediately when you switch away. Close the tab. Hands back on the work. Complete the next concrete step. “I drifted. I’m back. Keep fucking going.”
Give the hard work a finish line

Choose one thing to finish today that changes your situation. Define “done” before starting.

  • Writing. Publish the essay, or finish the draft through the last paragraph.
  • Money. Open the IRS notices, record the tax years and balances, and make the call.
  • Photography. Make the edit, sequence the photographs, and export the set.

Attack the part you keep working around. Keep polishing the opening? Write the ending. Keep organizing the folder? Open the notice. Keep tweaking the edit? Make the final cut.

Put the task in front of you. Work for 20 minutes. Assess what happened and choose the next step toward completion.

At day’s end, write two lines. What did I finish? What thought did I act through?

I can feel the resistance and fucking love it and run towards the cliff and the friction and discomfort. Do the hard thing. Run to the pain. Finish it.

Friction maxxing plan

Focus on tomorrow’s reward instead of today’s discomfort. Make the reward concrete. Practice approaching the friction, finding satisfaction in the effort, and wanting to return.

  1. Make tomorrow’s reward vivid. Write what finishing today makes possible tomorrow. “The listing will be live. Buyers can find it. This obligation will be off my back.” Picture that result. “I fucking want that. Take the next step.”
  2. Name today’s discomfort. Be precise. “I’m afraid of choosing the price.” “I’m bored.” “This paragraph feels clumsy.” Rate its intensity from 0–10. Then ask, “Can I carry this feeling while I work for ten minutes?” Run the experiment.
  3. Choose productive friction. Identify the difficult action that directly advances the outcome. Submit the application. Finish the ending. Make the call. “There’s the part I’ve been avoiding. I’m going straight at it.”
  4. Stay through the first urge to escape. Catch the prediction. “I can’t stand another minute of this.” Test it by completing one more concrete step. Then check what happened to the urge. “Feel the pull. Stay. Finish this step.”
  5. Notice satisfaction during the effort. When something becomes clearer, sharper, or more complete, pause long enough to register it. “Fuck yes. I’m gaining ground.” Find the actual pleasure in solving, deciding, creating, and following through.
  6. Let completion register. Look at the finished result. Write what it makes possible and what felt rewarding. “Starting was rough. Figuring it out felt good. Having it finished feels fucking great.” Give yourself a specific experience to look forward to next time.
  7. Return tomorrow and use the evidence. Before the next demanding task, read yesterday’s observation. “I wanted to escape. I stayed. I made progress.” Choose the next challenge and a workload you can repeat. Protect the sleep, meals, movement, and breaks that support that commitment.

Keep the record honest. Some sessions will stay unpleasant. Build your appetite around the satisfaction you actually discover.

Want tomorrow’s reward.
Move toward today’s friction.
Feel the resistance. Lean fucking in.
Find the satisfaction.
Finish it. Feel it. Come back hungry.
