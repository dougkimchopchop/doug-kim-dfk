Doug Fucking Kim gets it done.

Use that line once, with your hands on the work. Then make the first move before you can turn it into another thought.

Doug Fucking Kim gets it done.
Say it once. Then put your hands on the keys — not after you feel ready, before.
The Practice
  1. Give the identity a physical cue. You know what changes when the Leica is in your hand. For writing, use both hands on the keyboard, the actual draft open, and your eyes on the sentence where you left off. Say the line once. Type.
  2. Decide what "done" looks like before you start. This morning, take a piece you've already started and bring it to a complete first draft. Give it the ending you've been putting off. Read it through once and save it.
  3. Practice at the point where you usually let yourself off. When "I'll finish it later" shows up, put your hands back on the keys and write the sentence you were about to leave unwritten. If you've already switched tabs, come straight back. That return is part of the practice.
  4. Keep the evidence private and literal. Write one line when you finish. "I finished the draft of ___." Tomorrow, use the same cue on the next obligation you've chosen for yourself.
