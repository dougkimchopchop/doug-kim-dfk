Writing Strategic Plan

Three assets that build on each other: a Substack people pay for, a stack of real bylines, and a book proposal an agent can sell.

Mission · The next chapter (draft — rewrite in my words) I'm becoming a writer and photographer whose work is published, read and paid for: essays and images about the strangers who let me into their worlds. I show up every day with discipline and urgency. I finish what I start and put it out into the world, until my voice matches my taste.

Direction: travel essays + original photography, centered on encounters with strangers. One body of work feeds the Substack, the pitches, the zines and the book. Anything that doesn't feed it waits.

The flywheel: Substack is the workshop and the platform → best pieces become bylines → bylines send readers back → strongest essays + proof of audience become the book proposal.

The book: encounters with strangers who let me in: the shepherd and the Korean ringtone, the man with the clocks, crashing the birthday party in Bucharest, the boy in Alexandria, the ferries, Kythira. The center to find: what am I looking for when I let a stranger take me somewhere? Every backlog essay published on Substack is a rough chapter.

Discipline
1
Same start, every time. Phone in another room → open the doc → reread the last paragraph → write one sentence.
2
Decide in advance. Make sessions are recurring calendar events. No deciding in the moment.
3
Stop mid-sentence so tomorrow's start is free.
4
Borrow urgency. Stack external deadlines: Thursday, submissions, workshop dates, a reader waiting.
5
Someone's watching. Weekly check-in where you say what shipped, out loud.
Deadlines · never miss Thursday
1
Two in the bank. Thursday publishes from finished work, never this week's draft.
2
Alternate formats. Essay (1,500–3,000 words, the book chapters) / Dispatch (one photo + 300–500 words, ~2 hours).
3
Wednesday 9pm rule. Not scheduled by then → publish a Dispatch.
Finishing
1
Max 2 pieces in progress. New ideas go in the pipeline, not a new doc.
2
Three drafts, then out. Get it down → find the center ("this is really about…") → line edit + read aloud. No Draft 4 without outside feedback.
3
Kill or ship. Untouched 3 weeks → decide on Sunday.
4
Count finishes, not words.
Reading · the input side

Reading is how your taste gets sharper and how your voice catches up to it, so it's tracked like the writing. 30 minutes a day, 6 days a week. On a bad day, 10 pages counts.

Every week
6 reading sessions · ~150 pages · 3 pieces from target venues (feeds the Venue Briefs) · 1 craft study
Craft study
One essay read twice, the second time with a pen: opening, turn, ending, how the images work, and one move to steal. Try it in the next Dispatch.

Tailoring to a venue
Never changes
My eye, the encounter, the sentence-level voice, what the piece is really about.
Changes
Length, context, the "why now", structure, how much of me is on the page, whether photos lead.
Venue Brief
Read 5 recent pieces → length · how much "I" · why-now hook · opening move · ending move · photos · service element · 3 pieces I'm most like · my cut.
Practice
One venue cut a month. Substack keeps the loose cut.
First Flags · small yeses

Micro-essays (NYT Tiny Love Stories, Metropolitan Diary) · photo open calls · flash nonfiction contests · guest posts on peer Substacks · Brooklyn and local publications. They count toward the 2 pitches a week. 3 small yeses by Dec 19. Verify current guidelines before each one.

Feedback
Now
Readers Circle of 3–5. Every draft goes out with 3 questions: Where did you get bored? What image stuck? What's it about?
Workshop
Small nonfiction workshop — Sackett Street or Gotham Writers. Check current sessions.
Trade circle
Monthly swap with 1–2 writers, written notes back.
Photo + text
Portfolio reviews, e.g. Photoville. Watch for the next window.
Substack
End each post with one specific question. Reply to everything.
Zines · Encounters

One zine per place, built from a backlog essay and its photos. Issue 1: Istanbul — The Ferries. ~24 pages, 25–50 copies. Tests whether the photos and text work together, and tests the book chapter. Use it as a leave-behind for editors and agents, a paid perk, and to sell at fairs like the NY Art Book Fair. Issue 1 printed by Feb 28.
